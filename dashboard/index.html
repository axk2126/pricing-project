Pricing strategy 2027 · Price Management, ZSEM · case 05

No monthly fee. Pay per use. Two plans.

Novčić Pay gives 40,000 people aged 18–26 a euro account and a debit card, and after a free launch year none of them pays anything. From 2027 the account has to pay for itself. This is the price list that does it, and the evidence behind it.

The problem
−€1.28M

a year today. 40,000 customers, nobody pays. Every segment costs more than it earns, and 56,000 free cash withdrawals a month cost more than all card income.

The recommendation
€0 · €3.99 · €6.99

Keep the account at no monthly fee and charge per use for the two things that cost money: €1.50 per cash withdrawal, 1.5% above the ECB rate. Sell two optional plans, Plus and Travel.

The result
+€176k

profit in 2027 if the survey holds, with 94.6% of customers kept. Stress-tested on five assumptions at once: median €53k, 86% chance of at least breaking even.

From a loss on every customer to a contribution of €876k

Today the account earns 0.2% of card payments and pays €1.00 every time a customer withdraws cash. The price list charges for exactly the services that cost money.

What each part of the price list adds, per year

DecisionThe withdrawal fee alone lifts the contribution from −€581k to above zero. Currency fees and the two plans then build the €876k that covers €700k of fixed costs.

Contribution = income minus variable cost. 2,160 customers (5.4%) leave; each of them lost money today, which is why the last step is positive.

Workbook · Stress test

Three findings that carry the recommendation

01 · StructureA monthly fee on the account is the wrong lever

If the account itself had a monthly fee, 55% of students, 25% of employees and 10% of travellers would leave: 16,600 customers. With fees per use and the two plans, 2,160 leave. For a student the next-best account costs about nothing, so there is no value to charge for.

DecisionBasic stays at €0 a month. The account earns through use.

02 · Price levelWillingness to pay sits at the reference value

An employee pays €3.00 a month on Basic for the withdrawals Plus includes; half would pay €2.99 for Plus. A traveller’s bill at the competitor is €8.00; 70% would pay €6.99, 30% €8.99. Plus is priced above its reference value because non-buyers stay and keep paying per use. Travel is priced below because 70% of non-buyers leave.

DecisionPlus €3.99, Travel €6.99. No neighbouring surveyed price earns more.

03 · RiskCash is the profit engine and the main exposure

Withdrawal fees bring €642k against €633k of withdrawal cost. Profit falls to zero if Basic customers avoid 82% of their withdrawals, or if the cost per withdrawal rises from €1.00 to €1.28 and the fee does not follow.

DecisionTrack withdrawals per customer from the first month; the fee is the lever that follows cost.

What the numbers do not prove

The base case is close to the best case. It takes the survey at face value, although stated willingness to pay usually overstates purchases, and it assumes customers withdraw cash as often at €1.50 as they did for nothing. The case has no data on either. The recommendation therefore rests less on the €176k than on two results that hold across the whole simulation: it beats one monthly fee in 85% of draws, and no alternative breaks even more often.
