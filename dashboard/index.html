Five ways to charge the same 40,000 customers

DecisionKeep the account at €0, charge per use for cash and currencies, and sell two optional plans. It earns €106k more than one flat fee and keeps 14,400 more customers.

Contribution per year. Today the company loses €581k before fixed costs; the largest single leak is cash: 56,000 withdrawals a month at €1.00 each, with no fee.

Three things to understand in one minute

1 · A monthly fee on the account is the wrong lever

55% of students, 25% of employees and 10% of travellers would leave: 16,600 customers, 41.5%. Fees per use lose only 2,160 (5.4%). The same word, ‘fee’, hides a factor-eight difference in churn.

DecisionNo monthly fee on Basic. The account’s reference value for a student is close to zero (Fluxa’s free tier), so no fee can be justified by value.

2 · Plus must sit above €3.00

A typical employee pays €3.75 a month in per-use fees on Basic. Plus at €2.99 plus the currency fee is €3.74: every rational employee would switch and the plan would earn nothing extra. At €3.99 it captures half of the differentiation value and earns €62k more than no plan.

DecisionPlus at €3.99, with 3 withdrawals included as a quantity fence against heavy cash users.

3 · Travel is a penetration price and should be

A traveller’s next-best alternative costs €12.00 a month (Fluxa free tier €8.00 + insurance €4.00). Travel at €6.99 leaves €5.01 of surplus. The survey says 70% buy at €6.99 but only 30% at €8.99; €8.99 would win only above 37.1% take-up.

DecisionTravel at €6.99 and invest the surplus in value communication: show the €12.00 comparison on the site.

What could break it, and the answer the structure already contains

Sensitivity of the 2027 profit

DecisionThe withdrawal fee is the profit engine and withdrawal behaviour is the biggest unknown. Track withdrawals per Basic customer monthly (KPI) and keep the fee adjustable.
