Priced like the market, financed like the case.

What the benchmark changes, and what it does not

Three things taken from it

  1. The three-step ladder is what the market shows. A free tier, a low-priced middle plan and a travel-oriented top plan is the pattern customers already know. Plus at €3.99 sits at the price of the best-known middle plan; Travel at €6.99 sits under the best-known premium plan and includes insurance.
  2. Allowances instead of all-or-nothing. The real providers include a monthly amount of free withdrawals and charge above it. Plus does the same with three withdrawals, which keeps the heaviest cash users paying.
  3. A youth account at €0 is the norm. A bank’s own youth account without a maintenance fee confirms that Basic cannot carry a monthly fee.

Three things deliberately not copied

  • An account with no fees at all. Providers that offer it earn elsewhere: brokerage, interest, lending. Novčić has none of these in the case; copying the surface would recreate today’s loss.
  • A currency fee near cost on the free tier. Novčić’s cost is 0.3%. The 1.5% on Basic costs a student about €0.38 a month; heavy users get 0% in Travel.
  • Four or five tiers. Three segments, three offers. A fourth offer would need a segment the case does not have.

DecisionThe benchmark informs the shape and the level of the price list. What Novčić can afford is decided by the case: 0.2% card income and €1.00 per withdrawal.
