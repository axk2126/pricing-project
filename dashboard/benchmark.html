What the benchmark changes, and what it does not

Three lessons taken

  1. The ladder is the market standard. €0 / €3.99 / €9.99 is what a Croatian 20-year-old already sees at Revolut. Plus at €3.99 is priced at the reference; Travel at €6.99 is positioned under Premium with insurance included, which is the one visible advantage.
  2. Allowances instead of all-or-nothing. Wise and Revolut give a monthly allowance and charge above it. Plus follows the same logic with 3 included withdrawals, which keeps the heaviest cash users paying.
  3. Small-withdrawal fees track cost. Trade Republic’s €1 under €100 recognises that a withdrawal costs the same whatever its size. The case gives no withdrawal amounts, so it stays an option for 2028 once Novčić has its own data.

What was deliberately not copied

  • Trade Republic’s €0 everything. Its account is paid for by brokerage and the interest spread; Novčić has neither in the case. Copying the surface would recreate today’s loss.
  • Wise’s 0.3% currency margin on the free tier. Novčić’s cost is 0.3%; a 0.3–0.5% fee would leave nothing. The 1.5% on Basic is accepted by light users (students pay €0.375 a month); heavy users get 0% in Travel.
  • Revolut’s five tiers. Three segments, three offers; a fourth tier would add complexity without a segment to serve (handout 04: each extra price point adds enforcement cost).

DecisionUse the benchmark to set the level and the shape, not to copy the business model. The case’s economics (interchange 0.2%, €1.00 cash cost) decide what Novčić can afford.
