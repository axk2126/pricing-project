Report part 2 · Pricing strategy

Three offers, two fees per use, no monthly fee.

The offers, the price metric and the conditions that decide who pays which price; then the level of each price between its floor and its ceiling, and the test of every neighbouring price.

DecisionTravellers gain by choosing Travel; students stay on Basic; employees buy Plus for what it adds. One student price (Plus at €2.99 with verification) was tested and rejected: it would add €8k a year, not enough for a buyer-identification fence.

Plus bill = €3.99 + 1.5% on other currencies. Ways around the fences: a heavy cash user on Plus pays €1.50 from the fourth withdrawal; a traveller who buys Travel only in travel months also causes no travel cost in the months at home; a student who never withdraws cash still contributes €0.20 a month.

Workbook · Arbitrage

One gap, and the safeguard

Cash withdrawals in Travel have no cap. From six withdrawals a month Travel is the cheapest offer for anyone, traveller or not (Plus then costs €8.49), and from eight it costs Novčić more than the customer brings. The case reports one to two withdrawals as typical, so the model contains no such customers, but the account data will show whether they exist.

DecisionWatch withdrawals per Travel subscriber from the first month. If heavy cash users move in, add a fair-use limit to Travel (for example five withdrawals a month, then €1.50), announced with the fee information document.

Price level: floor, ceiling and the price chosen

Value-based range and the price

DecisionPlus is neutral: €0.99 above its reference value, half of the differentiation value (the 50:50 starting point). Travel is a penetration price, €1.01 below its reference value. The difference is what a non-buyer does. An employee without Plus stays on Basic and still contributes €2.80 a month, so losing a subscriber costs only €1.07. A traveller without Travel leaves with 70% probability, so losing a subscriber costs €2.25.

Floor = reference value, ceiling = total economic value (Nagle & Müller, Ch. 6). The Plus ceiling is a survey proxy, not a calculated saving. Travel’s cost floor is €2.70.

Workbook · EVE · Break-even

Is a penetration price for Travel justified?

  • The survey sets the limit. Only 30% would pay €8.99, a price well inside the value range. Perceived value is far below economic value.
  • The margin allows it. A Travel subscriber contributes €4.29 on €6.99, a margin of 61%.
  • The rival is unlikely to respond. 4,000 travellers are too few for an international app to start a price war over.
  • The segment is worth keeping. Travellers bring the highest contribution per customer.

DecisionLaunch at €6.99. The price just under the reference value, €7.99, pays if at least 48.5% of travellers take it; a straight line between the survey points gives 50%. Too close to decide on paper, so it is the first price to test in 2027.

Would a neighbouring price earn more?

Breakeven sales change of each price move

Price moveMargin at stake per subscriberVolume change that keeps profit equalSurvey saysVerdict
Plus €3.99 → €2.99€1.07+1,351%+25%keep the price
Plus €3.99 → €4.99€1.07−48%−50%keep the price
Travel €6.99 → €5.99€2.25+80%+14%keep the price
Travel €6.99 → €8.99€2.25−47%−57%keep the price

DecisionNo. A price cut would need far more new subscribers than the survey offers; an increase would lose more than it may. Plus at €4.99 is the closest call (it may lose 48%, the survey says 50%), so the lower price with twice the subscribers is the safer choice.

Breakeven sales change = −ΔP ÷ (CM + ΔP) (Nagle & Müller, Ch. 9). The margin used is the incremental one: plan margin minus what the same customer would contribute on Basic, because a lost subscriber is usually not a lost customer. With the plain plan margin (€3.79 and €4.29) the thresholds would be +36% / −21% for Plus and +30% / −32% for Travel, and the verdicts are the same.
