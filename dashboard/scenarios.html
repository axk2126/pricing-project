One monthly fee for everyone: the trap in the survey

Profit of a flat fee at each level, with the survey churn held constant

DecisionAbove about €4 the curve is fiction: it assumes 45% of students stay although only 4% would pay €4.99 for a richer plan. In the credible range (€2.99–3.99) the flat fee earns at most €70k and loses 16,600 customers. Break-even fee €3.74.

Everything included (bank-style package): no per-use fees. If per-use fees were kept alongside, the break-even flat fee would be €0.05, which is another way of saying the per-use fees carry the company.

Sensitivity

Profit after fixed costs when one assumption moves

DecisionWithdrawal behaviour dominates. Measure it from month one and keep the withdrawal fee as the adjustable lever; the plan prices hold across every single-factor shock.

Combined downside (half the withdrawals, take-up −20%, features €0.25): €6k. Student fence: €184k. Plus €4.99: €156k. Travel €8.99: €162k.

What-if questions

Cost per withdrawal rises to €1.50

Profit falls to €-141k: the margin on 52,800 monthly withdrawals disappears.

Raise the withdrawal fee to €2.00 (Fluxa charges €2 abroad; banks €1–2) and explain it as a cost pass-through: profit €73k at unchanged churn.

DecisionPass the cost through, say why, keep the €0 account.

Interchange cap halved to 0.1%

Interchange income halves; profit €-50k. Raising Plus to €4.99 does not help (take-up halves).

The withdrawal fee at €2.00 restores €164k.

DecisionPer-use fees, not plan prices, absorb regulatory shocks.

Fluxa drops its currency fee

The traveller’s reference value falls from €12.00 to €8.00; Travel at €6.99 still leaves €1.01 and the insurance.

Reactive breakeven: cutting to €5.99 costs €33,600 a year and pays only if more than 23.3% of subscribers would otherwise leave. Profit if cut: €149k.

DecisionHold the price; strengthen the insurance message; use the annual plan as the targeted give–get.
