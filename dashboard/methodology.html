Methodology · how the numbers were made

Mission to dashboard, one chain.

Every KPI follows from an objective, every price point from a KPI, every number from the case sheet. Two independent calculations agree to the cent.

The logic of the project

Mission / Vision→Strategic objectives→KPIs→Pricing strategy→Financial impact→Dashboard

From mission to dashboard

The chain follows the Balanced Scorecard logic taught in Quantitative Methods for Managers (Pirić): Mission → Values → Vision → Strategy → Objectives → KPIs → Targets → Initiatives. Pricing is the main initiative: each price point is chosen to move a KPI that follows from an objective, not the other way round.

The pricing itself follows the strategic pricing pyramid of Price Management (Nagle & Müller 2018): value creation → price structure → value communication → pricing policy → price level → competition.

How the model works

Each segment is followed through its choices

  1. At the chosen plan price, the survey share buys the plan (no interpolation between surveyed points).
  2. Everyone else stays on Basic and pays per use, except the survey share who leave because of per-use fees. That churn applies to those who actually pay the fees.
  3. Contribution = income (interchange 0.2%, plan fees, per-use fees) − variable cost (€0.60 account, €1.00 per withdrawal, 0.3% of currency payments, €0.50 insurance).
  4. Totals × 12 months, minus €700,000 fixed costs.
  5. Segments are independent, so the best price is chosen one segment at a time, then the structure is compared with the alternatives.

Verification

Two independent calculations

The workbook (live formulas, 11 sheets) and a Python script compute the same model from the same inputs. The workbook was recalculated headlessly and 25 totals were compared with the script: maximum difference €0.00. The dashboard is generated from the script, so every figure here is the same figure as in the workbook.

Limitations to say out loud

What the case cannot tell us

  • The churn for ‘a monthly fee’ was asked without a fee level, so the flat-fee alternative is only credible up to about €4.
  • No data on how withdrawal behaviour responds to a fee; this is the biggest sensitivity and the first KPI to watch.
  • No lifetime value: the case gives no ageing of students into employees, although that pipeline is the economic reason to keep students at €0.
  • Take-up is known at three price points per segment only; €7.99 for Travel is not modelled (linear interpolation would give ≈ 50% and €176k, marginally above €6.99).
