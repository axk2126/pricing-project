From the case sheet to the price list

The order of work

  1. Segments and value. What each segment costs and earns today; the economic value of each plan against its next-best alternative.
  2. Structure. Price metric, offers and fences, tested for self-selection.
  3. Price level. Floor and ceiling per offer; contribution at every surveyed price; breakeven sales change of each neighbouring price.
  4. Alternatives. The same customers under four other structures.
  5. Risk. One-way sensitivity, switching values, a simulation, three what-ifs.
  6. Presentation. Value message and the EU rules of the case.

The order follows the strategic pricing pyramid of Nagle & Müller (2018): value creation, price structure, value communication, pricing policy, price level.

How the model follows a customer

  1. At the plan price, the survey share of the segment buys the plan. No interpolation between surveyed prices.
  2. Everyone else stays on Basic and pays per use, except the survey share who leave because of fees per use.
  3. Contribution = income (0.2% of card payments, plan fee, fees per use) − variable cost (€0.60 account, €1.00 per withdrawal, 0.3% of currency payments, €0.50 insurance).
  4. × 12 months, minus €700,000 of fixed costs.
  5. Each segment is priced on its own. Students and employees buy the same Plus plan at two prices, separated by age.

One file, every number

Limits of the analysis

What the case cannot tell us

  • The churn for “a monthly fee” has no fee level. The one-fee alternative is therefore credible only up to about €4.
  • No behaviour after fees. How often customers withdraw cash once it costs €1.50 is unknown. It is the widest range in the simulation and the first number to measure.
  • Three price points per plan. Prices between them, such as Travel at €7.99, cannot be evaluated, only bounded: it needs 48.5% take-up.
  • Segment averages. A “typical month” hides light and heavy users within a segment; the adverse-selection test addresses the most costly case.
  • One year. The case gives no lifetimes, so the value of a student who becomes an employee is argued, not calculated.
  • The simulation’s ranges are judgment. They show how the structures compare under the same doubts, not the true probability of a loss.
