What this shows

Contribution today is −€580,800 a year; with fixed costs the loss is −€1,280,800. Cash is the largest leak: 56,000 withdrawals a month cost €672,000 a year, more than the €480,000 earned on card payments.

DecisionCharge for cash and for other currencies per use. These are the two costs that vary with what a customer does; the €0.60 account cost is covered by card income for employees and travellers and nearly for students.

Workbook · Segment economics

How they react to fees

Customers lost under each kind of fee

DecisionA monthly fee on the account loses 41.5% of the base, the recommended structure 5.4%. This one comparison rules out a flat fee and makes the €0 account the base of the structure.

Monthly fee: 55% of students, 25% of employees, 10% of travellers leave. Per-use fees: 5% / 3% / 70% of those who pay them. Plan buyers do not pay them, so only non-buyers are exposed.

Workbook · Model · Alternatives

What losing 41.5% would mean

  • 23,400 customers remain. With everything included, the fee that just breaks even on them is €3.74; at €3.99 the profit is €70k.
  • It is one survey answer, not a demand curve. The survey did not say at which fee people leave. Above about €4 the figure is unusable: only 4% of students would pay €4.99 even for a plan with extra features.
  • It gives away the next segment. 13,200 of the leavers are students, who become the employees of the following years. A retained student contributes €11 a year, a retained employee €38: students are worth more as next year’s employees than as this year’s customers.

DecisionKeeping the students is an objective in its own right. The €0 account is what it costs.

What they would pay for a plan

Students · Plus features

Free cash withdrawals, shared accounts, virtual cards. Thin demand: 15% at €2.99, 8% at €3.99.

Employees · Plus features

Half would pay €2.99, 40% €3.99, 20% €4.99. Demand halves above €3.99.

Travellers · Travel features

No currency fee, withdrawals worldwide, insurance. 70% at €6.99, 30% at €8.99.

Economic value of the Travel plan

Economic value = reference value (the price of the next-best alternative) plus the value of what the offer adds to it (Nagle & Müller 2018, Ch. 2).

Reference value, differentiation value and the surveyed prices

Typical month of a traveller: €800 of card payments, €400 of them in other currencies, two cash withdrawals abroad. The alternative is Fluxa without Premium, the cheapest way to do the same elsewhere.

In both segments about half of the customers accept a price near the reference value, and far fewer accept one above it. The reference value is what customers reliably perceive; the differentiation value (the insurance, the extra features) is perceived by a minority.

DecisionEconomic value says what an informed traveller would pay: up to €12.00. The survey says what travellers perceive today: about €8.00. The price is set on the survey, and the website communicates the €12.00 to close the gap.
