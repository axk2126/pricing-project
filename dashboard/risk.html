Workbook · Stress test · block C

The same draws applied to the alternatives

DecisionThe recommendation earns more than one fee for everyone in 85% of the draws. One fee has the widest spread: it spreads €700,000 of fixed costs over 23,400 customers, so every change in churn hits hard.

For one fee, the churn factor scales the monthly-fee churn (55 / 25 / 10%) and the usage factor scales everybody’s withdrawals, which are then included for all.

If the wrong customers buy Plus

Adverse selection, with total withdrawals held constant

The model lets a Plus subscriber withdraw as often as the average of the segment. More likely, the customers who buy a plan with included withdrawals are those who withdraw most. If every Plus subscriber among the employees uses the full three withdrawals (students: two), the customers left on Basic withdraw correspondingly less and pay fewer fees. Profit falls from €176k to €57k.

DecisionThis is the reason for “3 withdrawals included” instead of the survey’s “free withdrawals”. With a cap the loss is bounded and the price list still breaks even; without one it would not be.

Workbook · Stress test · block B

Three what-ifs

A cost risesEach withdrawal costs €1.50 instead of €1.00

With unchanged fees the profit turns into −€141k: the margin on 52,800 paid withdrawals a month is gone.

Raising the withdrawal fee to €2.00, the level Fluxa charges abroad and the top of the banks’ range, brings it back to €73k at unchanged usage and churn.

DecisionPass the cost into the fee that carries it, say that the cost rose by €0.50 and the fee with it, and leave the €0 account alone.

A rule changesThe EU halves the cap on card income to 0.1%

Card income halves and the profit becomes −€50k. Raising Plus to €4.99 would not help: take-up halves.

The withdrawal fee at €2.00 restores €164k.

DecisionFees per use, not plan prices, absorb a regulatory shock.

A competitor movesFluxa drops its currency fee

The traveller’s reference value falls from €8.00 to €4.00 and the economic value from €12.00 to €8.00. Travel at €6.99 still costs less than Fluxa plus separate insurance.

Matching with a cut to €5.99 costs €33,600 a year and pays only if more than 23.3% of Travel subscribers would otherwise leave. Profit after such a cut: €149k.

DecisionHold the price and lead with the insurance; offer the annual plan to those who ask for less.

If customers also avoid withdrawals, the fee keeps its effect: with 25% fewer withdrawals on Basic the profit is €123k at €1.50 and €283k at €2.00, before any further reaction to the higher fee.
